Before you start

Have these five things ready and the whole form takes about fifteen minutes.

A Google Account

The account you sign in with will permanently own the developer account.

An email address

Shown publicly on your Developer Profile. You verify it with a code.

A contact phone

Private — used only by Google. Include your country code (e.g. +91).

A credit / debit card

For the one-time US$25 registration fee (≈ ₹2,382 in India).

A valid photo ID

For identity verification, which Google requires before you can publish.

Which account type should you pick?

Both can earn money on Google Play. The right choice depends on whether you're a business or an individual.

An organisation

company · nonprofit · government · school
  • Best for a registered business or organisation
  • You must obtain a D-U-N-S number (free to request, but can take days)
  • Google verifies the organisation
  • Required to publish finance, health, VPN or government apps
  • Full legal address shown publicly if you sell / earn money

Yourself

individual · hobbyist · student
  • Best for an individual developer
  • No D-U-N-S number required
  • Fastest route to a live account
  • Can still earn money and invite team members
  • A few app types can only be published by organisations
Not sure? If you're publishing under your own name and don't run a business, choose Yourself. The screenshots below show this path, with the organisation option highlighted alongside it.

You're done — here's what happens next

  1. Payment confirms your account. Once the US$25 goes through, your developer account is created.
  2. Google verifies your identity. You may be asked to upload a valid photo ID. Verification can take a few days — you'll get an email when it's complete.
  3. Start publishing. Open Play Console → Create app, fill in your store listing, upload your app bundle, and roll it out to the Play Store.
Tip: keep your legal name, address and ID consistent everywhere. Mismatches are the most common reason verification stalls.
