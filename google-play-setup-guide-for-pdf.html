Google Play Console · Developer onboarding

How to open a Google Play developer account

A short, click-by-click walkthrough of every screen Google shows — from choosing an account type to paying the one-time fee — so you can start publishing on the Play Store.

  • One-time feeUS$25 ≈ ₹2,382
  • Time to fill in~15 min
  • Setup steps10 + payment
  • After payingID verification

Before you start

Have these ready and the form takes about fifteen minutes.

  • A Google Account — it permanently owns the developer account.
  • An email address — shown publicly on your profile; verified with a code.
  • A contact phone — private to Google; include your country code (+91).
  • A credit/debit card — for the one-time US$25 fee (≈ ₹2,382 in India).
  • A valid photo ID — for the identity check required before publishing.

Choose your account type

This is the only fork. After it, both paths follow the same steps.

B Yourself

  • For an individual, hobbyist or student
  • No D-U-N-S number needed — the quicker route
  • Can still earn money and invite others

A An organisation

  • For a company, nonprofit, government or school
  • Requires a D-U-N-S number (mandatory)
  • Needed for finance, health, VPN & government apps
1

Choose an account type

→Pick An organisation or Yourself, then click Get started.
  • The Google account shown top-left will own this account permanently — pick the right one now.

A If you chose “An organisation”

B If you chose “Yourself”

2

Review “What you'll need”

→Read the checklist, then click Continue.
  • A public email, a private contact phone & email, and a payment method for the US$25 fee.
3

Android developer identity

→Nothing to type — just read and click Next.
  • Google reuses your legal name, address and website to meet Android verification requirements.
4

Developer name

→Type the public developer name shown under your apps, then Next.
  • This is a single text box (no screenshot). Use your own name or brand — you can change it later.
5

Link a payments profile

→Click Create or select payments profile, complete it, then Next.
  • A Google payments profile confirms your legal name and address. Next stays greyed out until it's linked.
6

Public Developer Profile

→Confirm your details, set the public email, tick the box, then Next.
  • Your legal name, country and developer email are shown publicly on Google Play.
7

About you

→Optionally add your experience, answer the “other accounts” question, then Next.
  • The experience box is private (only you see it). Say whether you've used other Google Accounts for Play Console.
8

Apps

→Answer a few planning questions, then Next.
  • How many apps, whether you plan to earn money, and any special categories. These answers don't limit what you can publish.
9

How Google contacts you

→Enter your private contact details, then Next.
  • Contact name, email (verify it), language and phone with country code. All private — never shown on Google Play.
10

Accept the Terms

→Tick the two required agreements, then Create account and pay.
  • Tick the Developer Distribution Agreement (confirms you're 18+) and the Play Console Terms of Service. The other two boxes are optional.
⚠Heads up: the US$25 fee is non-refundable if Google can't verify your identity — make sure your legal details match your ID.
₹

Pay the one-time US$25 fee

→Click Add credit or debit card, enter your card, and complete the payment.
  • It's a one-time registration fee, not a subscription. In India that's about ₹2,382 at ₹95.27/US$ (26 Aug 2026); the exact amount depends on the day's rate.

You're done — what happens next

  1. Payment confirms your account. Once the US$25 goes through, the account is created.
  2. Google verifies your identity. You may upload a photo ID; this can take a few days, and you'll get an email when it's done.
  3. Start publishing. Open Play Console → Create app, add your store listing, upload your app bundle, and roll out.
Steps and screenshots reflect the Google Play Console sign-up flow captured on 26 August 2026; Google occasionally updates this wizard, so a screen may look slightly different. The US$25 fee is set by Google; the rupee figure is an approximate conversion.
